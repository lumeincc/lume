How a message travels

  1. Encrypted

    On the sender's phone, with a key only the two devices have.

  2. Sealed

    The sender's name goes inside the envelope. Outside, only the recipient.

  3. Waits on the server

    Until the recipient connects, and never longer than 30 days.

  4. Decrypted

    Only on the recipient's device.

  5. Key deleted

    A used key is erased at once.

How LUME is built

Account

No phone number or e-mail

Your account is created on your device from a 24-word recovery phrase. LUME never asks for a phone number, an e-mail address or a name, and the phrase never leaves your device.

Encryption

On the device

The sender encrypts a message and the recipient decrypts it. The server has no keys.

Keys

Post-quantum exchange

A conversation starts with two key exchanges, X25519 and ML-KEM-768. Reading it would take breaking both.

Code

Open to inspection

The code is public under the GNU AGPL. The cryptography comes from libsodium and liboqs, and every construction is checked against published test vectors.

What LUME protects and what it does not

Protects

  • What you write, against our server, the network and anyone who later gets the server's storage, including someone who records traffic today and has a quantum computer later.
  • Old messages, if your device is taken later: a message key is deleted once it has been used.
  • Your conversations after a break-in: someone who stole a conversation's keys once and then lost access is locked out again within one round of messages.
  • Who sent a message: the server stores whom a waiting message is for, never whom it is from.
  • Your groups: the server holds no groups, group names or member lists.
  • What is stored on your device, against someone who has the device but not your passphrase.

Does not protect

  • Your IP address and timing. The server sees where every connection comes from, and someone watching it live can match senders and recipients. Use Tor or a VPN if that matters to you.
  • How much you receive and when.
  • Whether a username exists.
  • A stolen recovery phrase. Whoever has it can act as you.
  • Some recordings, for a limited time, if your device is taken: the first messages of some conversations for up to 40 days, and keys of messages that arrived out of order for up to 14 days.
  • A compromised device. Malware running as you reads what you read.

What is ready

The specification is a draft, revision {{spec-revision}}, and can still change.

Ready

  • The cryptographic core in C++, on libsodium and liboqs, with a self-test at start-up
  • Accounts from a 24-word recovery phrase
  • Safety numbers
  • The hybrid post-quantum handshake
  • The Double Ratchet with encrypted headers
  • The binary wire format, with a fuzz target for every parser
  • Sealed sender and padding of message lengths
  • Usernames checked without asking the server

In progress

  • Encrypted storage on the device
  • The relay server
  • The Android app
  • The Windows app
  • An independent security audit
